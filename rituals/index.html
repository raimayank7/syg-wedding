Indian wedding rituals explained

India has many ways of getting married, and a guest at one may be a stranger at another. These pages set out the ceremonies of eight traditions in the order they happen, in plain words.

TraditionThe central momentUsual time
Hindu wedding (North India)Pheras around the sacred fireEvening or night
Sikh wedding (Anand Karaj)Four laavan around the Guru Granth SahibMorning
Muslim wedding (Nikah)Nikah: consent before witnessesDay or evening
Christian weddingVows and rings in churchDay or evening
South Indian Hindu weddingTying of the thali at the muhurthamMorning
Bengali weddingSaat paak, shubho drishti and sindoor daanEvening
Marathi weddingAntarpat and mangalashtakaMorning
Gujarati weddingFour mangal pheraDay or evening

What most Indian weddings share

  • A promise before the wedding: roka, sagai, kurmai, mangni, nischayathartham or betrothal.
  • Turmeric and henna: haldi, maiyan, pithi, gaye holud, manjha and mehndi, in almost every community.
  • The arrival of the groom with his family, and a welcome by the bride's family.
  • One central act that makes the marriage: steps around the fire, the laavan, the nikah, the vows or the thali.
  • A farewell to the bride's home and a welcome in the new one.
  • A feast.

For couples from two traditions

Many couples today hold two ceremonies, or blend rituals from both families. Sit with both sets of parents early, list the rituals each side holds dear, and build the plan around those. A marriage between people of different religions can also be solemnised or registered under the Special Marriage Act: see marriage registration.

Customs differ between families and regions. These pages are general guides, reviewed on 11 November 2026.
